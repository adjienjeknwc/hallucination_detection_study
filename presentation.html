Motivation & Background

The Hallucination Dilemma in Generative AI

The Fluency Trap

  • LLMs output grammatically flawless and authoritative responses.
  • Fluency creates a psychological illusion of factual reliability.
  • Standard decoding maximizes token likelihood, not ground truth.
  • Users cannot intuitively distinguish between real and fake facts.

High Real-World Stakes

  • Medical Diagnosis: Fabricating non-existent drug interactions.
  • Legal Practice: Inventing fictitious court case citations.
  • Finance & News: Misquoting company revenues and historical events.
  • Enterprise RAG: Directly contradicting uploaded PDFs.

The Detection Challenge

  • Detectors must flag errors at runtime without known ground truth.
  • Lexical word-matching fails on semantically valid paraphrases.
  • Deep transformers are computationally heavy and uncalibrated.
  • The Core Trade-off: Catching falsehoods vs. wrongly blocking valid answers.
Core Terminology & Definitions

Direct Contradiction vs. Unsupported Claims

Type A: Factually Contradicted (Direct Conflict)

  • Definition: The statement directly clashes with explicit evidence in the reference context.
  • Evidence: "Water boils at 100°C at standard sea-level atmospheric pressure."
  • LLM Output: "Water boils at 50°C under normal sea-level conditions."
  • NLI Output Class: CONTRADICTION (Logit 0).
  • Detection Ease: Relatively straightforward for cross-attention mechanisms to spot.

Type B: Unsupported Claims (Extrapolation)

  • Definition: The statement may or may not be true in reality, but cannot be substantiated by the passage.
  • Evidence: "Arthur's Magazine was an American literary periodical published in Philadelphia."
  • LLM Output: "Arthur's Magazine was the most popular magazine in Pennsylvania history."
  • NLI Output Class: NEUTRAL (Logit 2).
  • Detection Rule: In grounded RAG, unverified neutral statements must be treated as hallucination risk.
Literature Taxonomy (Section III)

Taxonomy of Detection Techniques

1. Lexical Classifiers

TF-IDF, n-grams, and word frequencies with logistic regression. Fast and lightweight, but surface-level.

2. Natural Language Inference

Cross-encoders modeling premise-hypothesis entailment. Directly evaluates factual support.

3. External Retrieval

Queries Google or vector databases to retrieve evidence before validating generated statements.

4. SelfCheckGPT

Samples multiple responses at temp > 0. High semantic disagreement between runs signals hallucination.

5. Predictive Uncertainty

Analyzes internal LLM token entropy and logits. Low confidence spikes flag hallucinations.

6. Claim Decomposition

Splits long text into atomic single-fact claims and verifies each fact independently.

Experimental Setup & Methodology

HaluEval Benchmark & Leakage-Resistant Split

The HaluEval QA Benchmark

  • Dataset: pminervini/HaluEval ('qa' config, 'data' split).
  • Volume: 10,000 raw HotpotQA Wikipedia records.
  • Paired Answers: Each question contains:
    • Ground truth right_answer (Label 0: Supported)
    • ChatGPT-generated hallucinated_answer (Label 1)
  • Expansion: Yields 20,000 balanced binary examples.

Leakage-Resistant Partitioning

  • The Flaw: Shuffling after answer expansion leaks question entities across splits, allowing models to memorize topics.
  • Our Solution: Split unique questions BEFORE answer creation:
    • Train: 7,000 questions (14,000 answers)
    • Validation: 1,000 questions (2,000 answers)
    • Held-out Test: 2,000 questions (4,000 answers)
  • Verification: Assertion confirms 0 overlapping question IDs.
Detection Architectures

Model Specifications: Lexical vs. Semantic

Model A: TF-IDF + Logistic Regression

  • N-Gram Range: Unigrams + Bigrams (1, 2) to capture named entities.
  • Vocabulary Cap: 50,000 max features.
  • Sublinear TF: Applies 1 + log(tf) to prevent frequent words from dominating.
  • Classifier: Logistic Regression, max_iter=1000, balanced weights.
  • Input: Composite: Knowledge + Question + Candidate Answer.

Model B: DeBERTa-v3 NLI Cross-Encoder

  • Model: cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-small (SNLI & MultiNLI).
  • Premise: Reference Knowledge context.
  • Hypothesis: Candidate Answer alone (isolates the factual claim from question phrasing).
  • Cross-Attention: Full joint self-attention across premise and hypothesis.
  • Rule 1 (Argmax): Entailment → 0; Contradiction & Neutral → 1 (Risk).

Why Did TF-IDF Get Higher Accuracy Than DeBERTa?

TF-IDF was trained on 14k in-domain HaluEval examples, allowing it to capture ChatGPT's stylistic perturbation artifacts. DeBERTa was used zero-shot; its neutral predictions on short noun-phrase answers were mapped to hallucination risk, depressing accuracy but boosting safety recall.

Section V-C • Entailment Thresholding

Validation-Tuned Threshold Analysis (Rule 2)

Rule 2 Tuning on Validation Split

  • Decision Rule: Supported ONLY if P(entailment) >= tau; otherwise flag as Hallucination Risk.
  • Validation Sweep: Evaluated tau in [0.50, 0.95] on 2,000 validation answers.
  • Optimal Threshold: tau* = 0.95 achieved highest validation F1 (59.61%).
  • Validation Metrics at 0.95:
    • Acc: 44.06% | Prec: 46.65% | Recall: 82.56%

Diagnostic Test on 300 Sample (tau = 0.95)

  • Accuracy: 44.67%
  • Recall (Hallucinations): 84.67% (Catches 127 / 150 errors)
  • False Positive Rate (FPR): 95.33%! (Catastrophic penalty)
  • Specificity: 4.67% (Only 7 of 150 valid answers passed!)
  • Insight: Extreme thresholding turns the detector into a brick wall that rejects almost everything.
Error Audit • Usability Dilemma

The Safety vs. Usability Trade-Off

Rule 1: Argmax Decision Rule

  • True Negatives (Supported Kept): 20 / 150 (13.3%)
  • False Positives (Supported Rejected): 130 / 150 (86.7%)
  • False Negatives (Fake Missed): 34 / 150 (22.7%)
  • True Positives (Fake Caught): 116 / 150 (77.3%)
  • Takeaway: Conservative neutral mapping already penalizes valid answers.

Rule 2: Threshold tau = 0.95

  • True Negatives (Supported Kept): ONLY 7 / 150 (4.7%)
  • False Positives (Supported Rejected): 143 / 150 (95.3%!)
  • False Negatives (Fake Missed): 23 / 150 (15.3%)
  • True Positives (Fake Caught): 127 / 150 (84.7%)
  • Takeaway: Stopping 11 extra fake answers costs 13 extra valid answers.

Methodological Significance

Small benchmark samples (N=300) have wide error bounds. Evaluating on large partitions (N=4,000) is essential to establish genuine architectural superiority rather than random sampling noise.

Section VII • Study Limitations

Critical Methodological Limitations

1. Small Sample Size (N = 300)

Head-to-head comparison was restricted to 300 examples due to interactive Colab GPU limits. Larger test sets are required for enterprise deployment decisions.

2. Synthetic Benchmark Domain

HaluEval perturbations (ChatGPT on Wikipedia) reflect specific synthetic patterns. Results may not fully transfer to conversational or creative tasks.

3. Diagnostic Threshold Sample Reuse

The 300 sample was evaluated under both Rule 1 and Rule 2. While tuning was on validation, Rule 2 on the 300 sample is diagnostic, not an isolated final test.

4. Elliptical QA Hypotheses

Short answers like "Arthur's Magazine" lack subject-verb grammar, causing NLI models to predict Neutral rather than Entailment, hurting accuracy.

Future Roadmap • Production Design

Enterprise Architecture: Hybrid 4-Stage Pipeline

Stage 1: Atomic Claim Extraction

Decompose complex generative paragraphs into discrete, verifiable factual propositions.

Stage 2: Multi-Source Evidence Retrieval

Fetch live grounding text from internal vector databases, trusted knowledge graphs, and authoritative APIs.

Stage 3: Task-Fine-Tuned Cross-Encoder NLI

Evaluate atomic claims against retrieved evidence using cross-encoders fine-tuned specifically on QA pairs.

Stage 4: Calibrated Risk Triage & Human-in-the-Loop

Pass high-confidence answers automatically; route ambiguous borderline cases to human domain experts.

Summary & Conclusions

Key Takeaways for AI Engineers

1. Leakage-Resistant Evaluation is Non-Negotiable

Random answer splitting causes severe topical data leakage. Splitting by original question IDs is mandatory.

2. Lexical Baselines are Strong but Surface-Level

TF-IDF achieved 70.57% test accuracy by capturing benchmark artifacts, but missed over 40% of hallucinations.

3. Deep NLI Catches More Errors (+20.7% Recall)

Cross-encoders evaluate factual evidence directly, acting as a much stronger safety net in critical domains.

4. The False-Positive Penalty Must Be Measured

Optimizing recall in isolation can yield a 95% false alarm rate. Real-world systems must balance safety against usability.

Wrap-up & Discussion

Thank You! Questions & Discussion

Q: Why question-level split?

To prevent identical question entities from appearing in train and test, stopping models from cheating via topical memorization.

Q: Why did TF-IDF beat NLI in accuracy?

TF-IDF was trained directly on 14k in-domain answers; NLI was zero-shot and penalized on short noun phrases.

Q: What does tau = 0.95 show?

Extreme recall (84.7%) causes an unbearable false-positive penalty (95.3%), rejecting 143 of 150 valid answers.

Q: Why bootstrap CIs?

To quantify error margins (±5.7% on 300 sample vs ±1.4% on 4k), ensuring claims of superiority are statistically sound.
